# Housing Law

Review Procedures for Housing Development

# Short Description

An overview of multiple statutes governing processing of housing development land use applications by local governments.

# Long Description

## Limited Land Use Decisions
Limited land Review use decisions (LLUDs) are a specific category of land use applications in Oregon land use law. An excerpt from a LUBA Opinion provides useful context about limited land use decisions:
“As best we can determine, the 1991 limited land use decision legislation was intended to relieve local governments from having to comply with the complex procedural requirements applicable to quasi-judicial "land use decisions" and "permits," when making certain decisions on allowing permitted uses within urban growth boundaries. To that end, ORS 197.195(2) exempts limited land use decisions from the procedural requirements applicable to quasi-judicial "land use decisions" and "permits," and ORS 197.195(3) establishes a simpler set of procedural requirements for limited land use decisions. (Barrick v. City of Salem, LUBA No. 94-013; https://www.oregon.gov/luba/Docs/Opinions/1994/06-94/94013.pdf)
Applicability
ORS 197.015(12) specifically defines limited land use decision as:
Pertaining to a site within an Urban Growth Boundary
An application for:
Preliminary partition or subdivision plat
Site Review or Design review based on discretionary standards regulating a use permitted outright
Replat
Property line adjustment
Nonconforming extension, alteration or expansion
Review process
The limited land use decision statute at ORS 197.195 governs the review and approval of limited land use decisions.
Limited land use applications are not subject to quasi-judicial review (ORS 197.797)
The review must follow the applicable procedures in acknowledged comprehensive plan and land use regulations, and other applicable legal requirements
Local government must provide notice:
Sent to property owners within 100’ of the site and any recognized neighborhood or community organization that includes the site
Describes site and proposed development
Lists applicable standards and criteria and describes review process
Provides local government contact information, where to submit comments and comment deadline
Explains requirement that LUBA can only consider issues raised in writing during the comment period
Explains appeal rights
Requires the local government to provide an affidavit or other certification describing the notice given
Local government must provide a 14-day period for written comments prior to a decision
Written decision that explains the decision sent to applicant and anyone that commented
A local government may allow local appeal of a limited land use decision. Establishes options relating to the record and testimony/evidence provided in an appeal hearing as either on the record or de novo
A city can only use the procedures in ORS 197.195 to review a limited land use decision
A city can use a ministerial review process if the applicable land use standards do not require interpretation or the exercise of policy or legal judgment
Other information about limited land use decisions
Excluded from the definition of “land use decision” (ORS 197.015(10)(b)(C))
Excluded from the definition of “permits” (ORS 215.402(4)(a) & ORS 227.160(2)(a)). ORS 215 and ORS 227 contain statutes for “Planning and Zoning Hearings and Review”, and specific sections within those heading govern the review of permits and zone changes.
Defined as an “application” for purposes of the land use completeness and decision timelines in ORS 215.427 and ORS 227.178 (ORS 215.427(11)(b), ORS 227.178(12)(b)).
Appeals of limited land use decisions are within LUBAs jurisdiction (ORS 197.828)

## Specified Residential Development
ORS 197A.146 establishes an expedited process for review of certain zone changes, planned unit developments, and variances. It also amends LUBA’s jurisdiction to include these application types. 
Applicability
The procedural requirements apply only to a land use decision for residential development based on an application for: 
A zone change to allow a denser residential use designation
A planned unit development, or 
A variance from a residential approval standard
Note: These terms are not defined in statute. 
It further only applies to land that is: 
Inside the urban growth boundary (UGB), and  
“Zoned primarily for residential use or mixed residential use or planned for residential use” 
The procedural requirements do not apply to applications: 
That reduce minimum residential density of land 
For final subdivision or partition plat 
For a residential construction permit under the building code 
For final engineering review 
Subject to ministerial or other expedited approval procedure, including outright permitted uses 
Review process
Generally, the procedural requirements under this section are similar to limited land use decisions under ORS 197.195, but with several differences in the specific requirements imposed. 
These are not subject to quasi-judicial review (ORS 197.797) and must be reviewed under locally applicable land use regulations, except as provided under this section. 
Establishes specific procedural requirements for applications
Notice to property owners within 100’ of the site and any recognized neighborhood or community organization that includes the site
For zone change decisions that require a Post Acknowledgement Plan Amendment (PAPA) notice to DLCD, the local government provides the notice specified in this statute to DLCD and does not have to hold a hearing
Establishes specific requirements for the notice. This mirrors limited land use decision notice requirements in ORS 197.195.
Requires the local government to provide an affidavit or other certification describing the notice given 
Establishes requirements for local approval or denial of an application. This mirrors the limited land use decision statute (ORS 197.195). 
Requires the initial decision to be made without a hearing. Allows a hearing on appeal of the initial decision. Establishes options relating to the record and testimony/evidence provided in an appeal hearing as either on the record or de novo. Final decision must be provided to all participating parties. 
Other information about limited land use decisions
LUBA’s scope of review includes applications in ORS 197A.146(1) (ORS 197.830(6)(b); ORS 197.835(3)-(4))

## Review of Housing Applications
ORS 197A.400(5) and (6) establish procedural limitations for housing subject to clear and objective standards, conditions, and procedures.
Applicability
Applications subject to ORS 197A.400(1)
Review process
The local government:
May provide notice of the application only to property owners within 100 feet of the site, or within 500 feet for developments of 20 units or more
May not require a public hearing prior to making a decision on the application
May provide an opportunity for a local appeal only to the applicant.
Appeal to LUBA is available only for the applicant

## Opting into Amended Housing Regulations
State law sets parameters and timelines on the local review of housing. ORS 227.178 for cities and ORS 215.427 for counties outline the requirements and timelines that cities and counties must follow when reviewing applications for permits, limited land use decisions, or zone changes. One component of this statute is the goal-post rule, which clarifies that the city or county must apply the standards and criteria that were applicable when an application was first submitted.
SB 1537 amends the goal-post rule to enable an applicant for the development of housing to “opt in” to new standards adopted by a city or county that were adopted after the applicant submitted an application. This enables an applicant to apply a city or county’s new development standards and criteria if they choose to, without having to withdraw and resubmit their application.
This change applies to all permits and zone changes under ORS 227.175 and 215.427. It does not apply to building permits or other types of applications that are unaffected by the goal-post and 120-day rules set forth by these statutes.
An applicant may submit a request to apply newly adopted standards to a land use application any time after application submittal up to the issuance of public notice of the application. If an applicant requests to opt into newly adopted standards, the application would be deemed automatically incomplete and the timelines for completeness review and final decisions restart as if a new application were submitted.
The city or county determines what additional information, if any, is necessary to review the application under the newly applicable criteria. This process follows the typical completeness process, in which a city or county either deems an application complete or notifies the applicant in writing what information is missing and needed to render a decision.
A city or county may deny this request if the city has issued public notice of the application or if the applicant had previously requested to opt into newly applicable standards for a given application. Otherwise, a city or county is required to accommodate the request.ORS The city or county may not require the applicant to pay a fee, except to cover additional costs incurred by the city to accommodate the request. This means a city or county can charge for additional staff time or resources spent reviewing the application under the new standards but would not be permitted to simply charge the same fee twice if that did not reflect the cost incurred for review.
The city or county may require the applicant to provide additional information needed to render a decision under the new standards. This could include information resubmittal if the change affects the entire application, or the information is needed to understand the change in context. However, the city may not otherwise require the applicant submit an entirely new application or duplicative information.
The city or county may not require an applicant to repeat processes or hearings that are inapplicable to the change in standards or criteria.
